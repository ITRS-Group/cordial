# Middle East (ME) holiday calendars

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> These calendars were built with AI/LLM assistance and have not been validated by humans. Verify dates and suitability before use, and review the [refresh runbook](RUNBOOK.md).

Snapshot gathered **2026-11-20**. See the [overall holiday calendar guide](README.md) for coverage limits, source-confidence grades and scheduling examples.

No verified Middle Eastern calendars are included in this snapshot, so there is no ME XML include yet. This does not mean that the region has no holidays. Public, bank and exchange schedules need separate jurisdiction-specific sources. Where an authority publishes dates through later announcements, those announced dates are needed before overrides can be encoded; estimates are not treated as confirmed closures.

## Regional finance centres

The absence of encoded calendars is a coverage limit, not a lack of published
holiday information. Relevant markets include Bahrain (Manama), Kuwait
(Kuwait City), Qatar (Doha), Saudi Arabia (Riyadh), and the United Arab
Emirates (Abu Dhabi and Dubai). A country's public holidays, bank closures
and each exchange's non-trading days are distinct schedules; a public holiday
must not automatically be treated as an exchange closure.

## United Arab Emirates

### Public holidays

The [UAE government's public-holiday guide](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays)
publishes rules applicable to both public and private sectors, including
Dubai and Abu Dhabi, and links to the governing Cabinet resolution.
The source was checked on **2026-11-20** and is **Medium confidence**:
official human-readable HTML, rather than a machine-readable dated calendar.

The guide specifies Gregorian New Year on 1 January and National Day on
2 and 3 December. It also lists Eid Al Fitr, Arafah Day, Eid Al Adha,
Hijri New Year and the Prophet's birthday by Islamic-calendar date, explicitly
subject to moon sighting. Those rules alone do not provide confirmed Gregorian
dates for future years. The Eid Al Fitr duration also depends on whether
Ramadan has 29 or 30 days.

The guide states that a holiday coinciding with another holiday or a weekend
does not automatically transfer to another day. Gregorian dates, actual
announcements and any applicable transfer decisions must therefore be
distinguished; forecast dates are not suitable as confirmed full-day overrides.

### Exchange calendars

Dubai Financial Market, Nasdaq Dubai and Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange
are separate venues. Their dated trading notices must establish full-day
closures independently of the public-holiday rules above. No venue-specific
closure table has been verified for inclusion here, so no UAE XML calendar
has been added on the basis of the general government guide alone.
